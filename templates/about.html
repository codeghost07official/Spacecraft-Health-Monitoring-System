The problem

What is telemetry?

Telemetry is the stream of measurements a spacecraft sends home: temperatures, pressures, battery voltages, wheel speeds and many more. Think of it as the spacecraft's heartbeat and temperature chart, sent every few seconds.

There is so much of it that people cannot check every number quickly. A small problem hiding in the data can grow into a mission-ending failure. SHMS lets AI do the reading, so engineers can focus on fixing.

The AI model

Meet AERIS

Aerospace Equipment & Reliability Intelligence System. It learned from real NASA spacecraft data and works in three stages.

{{ icon("activity") }}

Stage 1 · The Forecaster

A Linear Regression model looks at the last 30 readings and predicts the next one. When the real reading is far from the prediction, that is a "surprise", an early sign of trouble. We measure it with MSE, RMSE, MAE and MAPE.

{{ icon("target") }}

Stage 2 · The Anomaly Detective

A Random Forest classifier studies 27 clues about each reading (its surprise, how rare it is, how it compares to the sensor's normal behaviour) and decides: normal or anomaly. It was picked after comparing it with Logistic Regression and a Decision Tree using 5-fold cross validation.

{{ icon("cpu") }}

Stage 3 · Sensor Mini Brains

Every NASA sensor has its own small neural network that learned only how that sensor normally behaves. When it is surprised, AERIS listens. NASA files are recognised automatically, even if renamed.

Words you will see

Anomaly
A reading or period that does not look like normal behaviour.
Precision
When AERIS raises an alarm, how often is it right?
Recall
Out of all real problems, how many did AERIS find?
F1 score
One number that balances precision and recall (1.0 is perfect).
MSE / RMSE
How far the forecaster's guesses were from the real readings. Smaller is better.
